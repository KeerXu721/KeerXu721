About

From meaning representation to agents in production

I started in computational linguistics — annotating meaning representations, arguing about what a predicate really entails, and asking how much of sentence meaning a model actually captures. That work turned into papers on AMR/UMR annotation and conditional semantic textual similarity at ACL and LREC-COLING.

Now I build the other half of that problem. At NetApp I work on an enterprise agentic AI platform — helping systems find the right information and act on it reliably. The linguistics still shows up daily: most retrieval failures are meaning failures wearing an infrastructure costume.

I hold an M.S. in Intelligent Information Systems from Carnegie Mellon's School of Computer Science, and a B.S. in Computer Science and Applied Mathematics from Brandeis University.

What I'm focused on

  • Retrieval quality Getting the right context in front of a model — the step that quietly decides whether an answer is correct.
  • Grounding Keeping generated answers tied to real source material, and making it visible when they aren't.
  • Evaluation Measuring answer quality properly, because "it looked good in the demo" is not a metric.
  • Meaning in practice Where linguistic structure still beats scale, and where it doesn't.

SWiT — Emoji Connotation Annotation

The same emoji can mean opposite things depending on context. We wrote annotation guidelines for three emotionally ambiguous emoji, labelled a corpus against them, and trained logistic regression and naive Bayes baselines to see how much of that connotation is recoverable from text.
